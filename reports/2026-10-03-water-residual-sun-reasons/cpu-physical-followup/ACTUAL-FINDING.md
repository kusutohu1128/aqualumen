## Ranked, falsifiable distinctions

1. **Unsupported forward Snell branch**: helper105–107 reason2 combines downward
   facet, facing, target-dot and positive finite discriminant tests. If exact
   captured inputs violate the forward transmitting support by a margin larger
   than a justified roundoff bound, relaxing a gate is wrong. Establish zero
   transmitted-source support independently, rather than label every rejected
   inverse point numerical TIR or discard the whole Sun replacement blindly.
2. **Critical cancellation**: for unit generated target `t`, raw observed `v`,
   `k²=1+eta²*(v·v−1)`, `a=k*t+eta*v`, `h=a/|a|`, exact arithmetic gives
   `D=1−eta²*(1−(h·v)²)=k²*(t·h)²` and
   `t·h=(k+eta*t·v)/|a|`. Thus the valid-branch condition `t·h<0` has a real
   boundary, while the current subtraction for D can lose relative accuracy
   near it. Generated binary32 targets are not exactly unit: the identity is
   a derivation aid, **not permission to replace D without accounting for their
   norm and operation/FMA roundoff**.
3. **Inverse reconstruction instability**: reason4 at helper111–114 includes
   finite/norm failures and the existing EPS8 inverse-error bound. Accurate
   positive-support reference plus float-only reconstruction disagreement would
   distinguish this from a genuinely absent branch. No tolerance widening now.
