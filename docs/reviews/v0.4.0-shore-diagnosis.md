## Ranked candidates and distinguishing probes

1. **Above-water refraction has a hard provenance switch.** `gbuffers_water.fsh:100–132` preserves the initial Snell estimate on failure and returns the original screen UV after only eight fixed-point iterations. Foreground-bank, above-surface, offscreen and nonconvergence exits share that fallback. The above-water caller ignores the miss kind and samples the opaque snapshot anyway (`210–226`). A valid refracted receiver/path therefore switches to an unrefracted color plus estimated path without a confidence transition. This is distinct from the already-fixed stale unconverged candidate commit. Probe the actual production main with identical scene inputs, changing only iteration budget 7/8/9 or a tiny depth perturbation at the convergence boundary. Record receiver kind, failure reason, path, residual and radiance. If visible bands do not track that switch, this hypothesis loses priority.

2. **The bottom is dry-lit before view-path extinction.** `lib/terrain.fsh.glsl:15` calls ordinary `terrainLight`; `lib/lighting.glsl:6–17` supplies air Sun/sky/block illumination. `deferred.fsh:17` preserves that shaded receiver; water subsequently applies view-leg transmission and caustics (`gbuffers_water.fsh:217–226`), not a reconstructed surface-to-bottom illumination path. Caustics currently multiply the entire receiver, including ambient/block light. A bright sand receiver plus differential extinction can look intensely cyan even with a correct trace. At the default clarity, a 6 m view path alone yields approximately RGB transmission `(0.147,0.600,0.811)` (`lib/water.glsl:22–24`). First inspect direct-Sun versus ambient/block receiver radiance separately; the planned direct-Sun MRT/caustic isolation is a useful bounded first change, not a complete underwater irradiance model. Do not add the same extinction twice to an already attenuated receiver. If hit paths and direct/ambient energy are plausible but hue remains excessive, extinction/scattering calibration becomes the next independent question.

3. **SSR can switch the reflected source independently of refraction.** `gbuffers_water.fsh:34–67` gates/refines depth hits and excludes receivers below `surfaceY-0.30`; misses use filtered environment radiance (`230–238`). A reconstructed hit can change discontinuously at geometry/visibility boundaries despite its residual/edge confidence. Probe SSR-hit classification and render an environment-only reflection control while preserving normals, Fresnel and transmitted color. If the serrated dark bands persist unchanged, SSR is not their primary source; inspect the point-normal/Fresnel field next rather than blaming the refraction trace by appearance alone.

4. **Shore depth is a screen-ray estimate, not local bathymetry.** The original opaque receiver height drives fragment wave attenuation and foam (`gbuffers_water.fsh:152,160,242`). Vertex displacement instead uses fixed depth 64 (`gbuffers_water.vsh:26`, `lib/waves.glsl:65`), while fragment shore attenuation changes over 0.035–0.8 m (`waves.glsl:56–59`). Thus an occluded bank or depth step can alter a water pixel's normal/foam when the camera moves, without corresponding surface geometry. Probe original-depth, refracted-depth, normal and Fresnel maps separately. This is a lower-priority explanation for the wide central cyan region: depths above 0.8 m already have full shore amplitude, so that taper alone cannot explain every band.

Tone mapping uses a shared RGB shoulder (`final.fsh:12–17`); actual water writes alpha 1 (`gbuffers_water.fsh:249`). Neither proves the water physically correct, but global tint/alpha changes should not precede these geometry/provenance probes. Above-water caustics also run on miss/proxy receivers (`219–222`); that must not be treated as a validated physical hit.

## Bounded trace/fallback improvement

Keep the 0.3.3 underwater sky, unknown-ray cave fallback, and TIR paths unchanged. Introduce explicit trace provenance: UV, path, receiver kind, failure reason, residual and confidence. An original-UV/Snell proxy stays labelled an approximation, never a successful hit. Do not restore the old long grazing straight-path initializer.

Shade a confirmed hit with its own receiver/path and shade the conservative proxy separately. Blend those **already attenuated radiances**, not UVs or optical lengths. Fade hit confidence to zero at convergence acceptance, screen-edge and bank/surface validity boundaries, retaining every existing hard rejection. This bounds the visible handoff without claiming offscreen geometry is known. Caustics require a proven receiver and should affect only the direct-Sun component of the planned new MRT, not ambient/block receiver light. A downward above-water ray without a bottom must not be reclassified as a successful sky receiver.

Increasing eight iterations alone cannot repair the caller's provenance semantics. If diagnostics establish nonconvergence as dominant, add a bounded ray march/bracket refinement only for failed solves; reject foreground/above-water and depth-discontinuity false crossings. This is a second change, independently tested, not an unbounded ray tracer or a new bathymetry system.

The native regression must exercise production main, not a duplicate tracer/color formula. Include the hard-switch reproduction; converged positive hit; offscreen and iteration exhaustion; strict foreground-bank rejection; above-water downward sky miss; and the existing near/deep/tilted underwater sky, TIR, cave and non-Earth controls. Preserve raw HDR output plus classification diagnostics so a display transform cannot hide an energy/provenance failure. Native GPU ownership remains with the root's assigned harness agent.
